### v4.5.3 (2026-08-17)

- **Eight rating-band gaps across the two frameworks, closed by a rounding rule (Sani ruling, 2026-08-17)**: `references/core-eeat-benchmark.md` § 3 and `references/cite-domain-rating.md` § 3 carried the identical scale — `90–100 Excellent · 75–89 Good · 60–74 Medium · 40–59 Low · 0–39 Poor`. Every endpoint is a whole number; every score either framework produces is a mean or a weighted mean and usually is not. Four windows per hundred therefore belonged to no band at all — anything strictly between 39 and 40, 59 and 60, 74 and 75, or 89 and 90 — four per framework, **eight in total**. Not theoretical: the preceding entry's blind grading recomputed a weighted total of **39.80**, and the deliverable it came from had printed the uncorrected 41.11 as "Low". **Sani ruled the fix is a rounding rule, not moved endpoints**: round the score to a whole number, half up, and read the band off the rounded figure. That makes the bands contiguous over the integers without touching a single endpoint. Encoded once in each framework's § 3, in the register of the precedent the library already held — `monitor/backlink-analyzer/references/link-quality-rubric.md` § 1 solved the same problem for the Link Quality Score in 4.3.0 (*"Round to one decimal, half up, and read the band off the rounded figure"*). **The precision relationship is stated on both sides so nobody harmonises them**: these bands round to a whole number because their endpoints are whole numbers, the LQS rounds to one decimal because its endpoints carry one decimal; one decimal here leaves 39.8 exactly where it was, and a whole number there collapses three bands into two. The rubric gains the reciprocal note, so the guard holds from either end.
- **Three things the ruling required to survive the edit.** (1) **The derivation stays visible** — both figures appear and neither replaces the other: the computed value with its arithmetic, at enough precision to show which side of a boundary it fell on, and the rounded value carrying the rating word (`39.80 → 40/100, Low`). Rounding is a reading step for the band alone; every recompute check still runs against the computed figure. (2) **Veto ordering, worked rather than left to inference** — round first, then cap, and read the cap and the band off the same rounded number. CORE-EEAT: a vetoed 59.6 rounds to 60, which stands above the cap, the cap binds, and the report says **59, Low**. CITE: a vetoed 39.6 rounds to 40, the cap binds, and the report says **39, Poor** with the Manipulation Alert. The entry states plainly that capping the unrounded figure first reaches the same number in both cases — that coincidence is *why* the order gets left to inference — and names the defect the ordering actually prevents: checking the cap against one figure and reading the band off another. A vetoed report printing 60/Medium has breached the cap however it got there. (3) **A chained-rounding hazard found while reconciling, and closed** — `domain-authority-auditor` reports the CITE Score at one decimal, so the band must come off the computed value rounded *once* to a whole number, never off the one-decimal figure rounded a second time: a computed 74.46 rounds to 74, Medium, but via 74.5 it becomes 75, Good. Stated in both frameworks and in that skill's own arithmetic reference, which already carried the same warning pointed at potential gains.

### v4.5.2 (2026-08-17)

- **domain-authority-auditor 4.5.0 (the potential-gain formula was the dimension formula with one factor dropped, 2026-08-17)**: `references/score-arithmetic.md` §5 stated `potential gain = recoverable points × that dimension's weight` while §3 of the same file defined `dimension score = points earned ÷ (10 × scored items) × 100`.
