# Mistral Dry D20 Dehumidifier Review: Six Weeks in a Damp Ground-Floor Flat

By Marta Ellinikou · Published 2 December 2025 · Updated 14 January 2026

## Verdict up front

After six weeks of daily use (15 October – 25 November 2025) in a 54 m² ground-floor flat in Kallithea, the Mistral Dry D20 brought indoor relative humidity from 78% down to a stable 55% and held it there. It is the first unit we have tested that stayed under 48 dB(A) in normal mode while still extracting more than 3 L/day once the flat had dried out. We recommend it for ground-floor and basement homes up to about 60 m².

## How we tested

We ran the D20 around the clock for 42 days, logging humidity with two calibrated hygrometers (living room and bedroom) and power draw with a plug-in energy meter. Readings were recorded every morning at 08:00.

- Week 1: average extraction 6.8 L/day at 78–70% relative humidity
- Weeks 2–3: 4.2 L/day at 70–60%
- Weeks 4–6: 3.1 L/day while holding 55%
- Measured power draw: 285 W in Turbo, 198 W in normal mode
- Noise at 1 m: 47 dB(A) normal, 54 dB(A) Turbo (phone-app measurement — see limitations)

## Two problems we hit

1. The float switch stuck once in week 3 and the tank-full alarm never sounded; the overflow cutout stopped the unit. Rinsing the float channel fixed it, and it has not recurred.
2. The drain hose kinks at the outlet. A 90-degree elbow fitting (€4 at any hardware store) solved it permanently.

## Limitations of this test

We tested one unit, in one flat, in autumn conditions. Noise was measured with a phone app rather than a class-1 sound meter, so treat the dB figures as indicative. We did not test continuous-drain mode beyond two weeks.
