## 1. METHOD AND LIMITS — read this before any number below

### What this is
22 Greek-language (and 2 English-language) web searches were run through a general web-search tool. For each query I recorded the domains that came back, what *kind* of page each was, and whether a client property appeared. That is the whole of the evidence base.

### What this is NOT
**This is not a measurement of what ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity or Copilot say, recommend, or cite.** I have no access to those answer surfaces. Nothing in this report may be restated as "ChatGPT recommends X" or "we appear in N% of AI answers" — that would be a fabricated measurement.

What it *is* is a proxy: a map of the open-web pages that currently surface for these queries, on the reasoning that answer engines draw their citable corpus from broadly similar open-web material. The proxy is one step removed from the thing you actually care about, and the gap is not quantified.

### Specific limits of this instrument
- **The search tool self-describes as US-localized.** Greek-language queries return Greek pages, but the ordering is not a Greek-localized SERP from a Greek IP. Do not read these as "Greek Google rankings".
- **No ranking positions are available.** The tool returns result blocks without rank numbers. Every "appeared / did not appear" below is binary presence in one result set. I have not written a single position claim, and none can be inferred from this file.
- **One sample, one day.** No repetition, no time series, no personalization control. A domain absent from one query is not established as absent from the category.
- **Result-set summaries are machine-generated.** Where I quote what a summary said, that is evidence about the result set, not an independent fact check of the underlying page.
- **No counts of anything.** No citation counts, no share-of-voice, no article counts, no traffic.

### What a decision would need instead
Any decision that turns on actual AI-engine behaviour — "are we cited in ChatGPT for αφυγραντήρες", "did our guide move Perplexity" — requires querying those products directly and logging their cited sources, or a tool that does so. This file cannot substitute for that and should not be cited as if it could.

Alongside that, price-comparison **editorial** (not listings): `bestprice.gr` buying guides, curated lists and FAQ pages; `skroutz.gr` articles and a short-video result. Manufacturer/brand blogs hold the sizing and FAQ queries hard — `inventoraircondition.gr` took two of the sizing-query results and also appeared on Q2; `morris.gr` appeared on Q5.

No appliance retailer appeared at all. Whether that is worth contesting is a strategy question, not a finding.
- Q9 (English) returned only US affiliate/commerce content (`wisedry.com`/`.net`, `aboutdarwin.com`, `bestdehumidifierchoice.com`). No Greek property of any kind. Given the tool's US localization this says nothing about whether a Greek-market English page could rank for Greek users.

## 3. Content-format finding — the actionable part

The brief's open question: the client's one proven AI-referral winner is a bilingual comparison buying guide; does that format dominate the other three categories too, or are they structured differently?

**Answer as far as this method supports it: three of four yes, heating no.**

| Category | Dominant page type on advisory queries | Client's appearing asset type | Format match? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Αφυγραντήρες | Independent "best of" / buying guides + comparison-site editorial + manufacturer blogs | Blog buying guides (4 different posts) | **Yes** — same format, client competes in it |
| Ανεμιστήρες οροφής | Buying guides and brand FAQ/blog content | One blog buying guide, carrying 3 queries | **Yes** — strongest single-asset performance observed |
| Πετσετοκρεμάστρες | Thin guide layer over heavy marketplace layer | Blog guide **plus** category page, often together | **Partly** — guide format works, but marketplaces are in every result set |
| Θερμοπομποί | **General news media and user forums** on comparison intent; retailer **category pages** on brand/browse intent | Mostly the category page; one calculator guide, plus a client microsite calculator | **No** — structurally different |

**The heating exception is the finding that matters.** In αφυγραντήρες and ανεμιστήρες, the pages competing for "which should I buy" intent are buying guides, so a better buying guide is a direct contest for the same slot. In θερμοπομποί, the pages holding the two highest-comparison-intent queries I ran («ή καλοριφέρ», «ή κλιματιστικό») were national news sites, an energy-comparison site, and two forum threads — and no client property appeared on either query. A retailer buying guide is not the same object as a `protothema.gr` explainer or an `insomnia.gr` thread, and publishing more retailer guides does not obviously displace them. That is a different content problem from the other three categories, and it sits in the second-largest revenue category.

Two secondary format observations:
- **The best-performing client asset per query is a single guide, not a fleet.** One fan guide appeared on three of four fan queries; one towel-rail guide appeared on four of five. Depth per asset, not asset count, is what showed up here.
- **Transactional and pure-sizing queries were the consistent client gaps** across categories: «πόσα λίτρα» (dehumidifiers, absent), «τιμές προσφορές» (towel rails, absent), «πόσα watt» for heaters (sanihellas absent; the client microsite covered it). Where a dedicated calculator-style page existed on a client property, it appeared. Where none existed, third parties held the query.

## 5. What I could NOT establish

This section is the honest floor of the report. Everything here is genuinely open.

1. **Anything at all about ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity or Copilot output.** Not what they answer, not what they cite, not whether any page above is in their citation set. No claim in this file may be relabelled as an AI-engine finding.
2. **Ranking positions.** The tool returns no rank. I have not stated one and none can be derived from this file.
3. **Any percentage, share, or count** — no share of voice, no citation counts, no "N of M queries" framed as a visibility rate, no article-count comparison against any competitor.
4. **Whether these results resemble a Greek-localized SERP.** The tool is US-localized. Greek pages come back, but ordering and inclusion may differ materially from what a buyer in Greece sees.
5. **Whether the client's English/bilingual assets rank for English queries in-market.** My two English queries returned US corpora. Their absence from those sets is not evidence of weakness.
6. **The three given competitive claims** — the dehumidifier competitor's AI-visibility lead, the heating competitor's publishing volume, and the towel-rail "de-branding" dynamic. None was tested; each remains as supplied context only. The heatovent and Inventor recurrences above are *consistent* with two of them, which is not the same as confirming them.
7. **Stability over time.** One sample per query, one day, no repeats, no personalization control. A domain's absence from one result set is not established absence.
8. **Which domain supplied the Tonon wattage text** in the towel-rail summary, and more generally the provenance of any machine-generated summary sentence.
9. **Whether any page observed is actually reachable by AI crawlers** — robots.txt, llms.txt, JSON-LD presence and rendering were not checked for a single domain here, including the client's.
10. **Any traffic, click, conversion or revenue consequence** of anything above.
11. **Content quality of competitor pages.** I recorded that a domain appeared and what type of page it was. I did not open, read, or assess a single competitor page.
12. **Query volume.** The 22 queries are plausible buyer phrasings chosen by me. Whether real Greek buyers type them, and at what volume, was not established — that needs GSC and keyword data, not this method.
