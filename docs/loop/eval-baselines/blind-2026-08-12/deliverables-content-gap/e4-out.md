## Prioritized Gap List

### How these were scored, and what was left out

**Search Demand was dropped, not guessed.** The factor is banded in monthly search volume; no tool is connected and nothing was supplied.

The obvious substitute — **competitor cluster depth** — is **not admissible here**, and the reason is worth stating: Competitive Density on every row below is scored from exactly that competitor coverage, and Density is inverted (5 = nobody covers it). One body of evidence feeding two factors in opposite directions at comparable weights makes them largely cancel, and five factors quietly behave as three. The ranking would look precise while carrying less information than it appears to.

Your own GA4 sessions on adjacent pages *would* be admissible — they are independent of the competitor crawl. They were not used either: adjacent-page sessions exist for the five depth gaps and for **none** of the two largest missing topics (no page of yours is adjacent to dogs or to winter), so proxying some rows and dropping the factor on others would put two differently-built numbers in one ranked list. Those session figures appear above as evidence to read; they are deliberately not scored.

**So the factor was dropped and the remaining four weights renormalised over their own sum (0.75):**

| Factor | Published | Renormalised | Direction |
|---|---|---|---|
| ~~Search Demand~~ | ~~0.25~~ | **dropped — no input** | — |
| Competitive Density | 0.20 | 0.20 ÷ 0.75 = **0.2667** | 5 = no competitor covers it |
| Business Relevance | 0.25 | 0.25 ÷ 0.75 = **0.3333** | 5 = core to your offering |
| Creation Effort | 0.15 | 0.15 ÷ 0.75 = **0.2000** | 5 = quick to create |
| Conversion Potential | 0.15 | 0.15 ÷ 0.75 = **0.2000** | 5 = decision/transactional |
| | | **Sum 1.0000** | All four **added**, none subtracted |

Every score is `(20·Density + 25·Relevance + 15·Effort + 15·Conversion) ÷ 75`, printed as an exact fraction in fifteenths beside the two-decimal rounded figure (halves up), with the tier read from the rounded figure.

**The quick-win screen was not run — Search Demand unavailable.** The Quick Win Score needs all four of its inputs and Search Demand is one of them; the three survivors would crown a gap nobody searches for merely for being cheap and uncontested. **No Quick Win Score appears anywhere in this report.** Gaps are ordered on the renormalised priority score alone and **P0 does the "start here" job** — so Tier 1 = P0, Tier 2 = P1, Tier 3 = P2 and P3, with no quick-win condition applied.

**One consequence**: Competitive Density's share rose from 20% to 26.7%. With no way to tell whether a crowded topic is crowded *because* it is in demand, "they are already there" is the strongest signal left — which is why winter and dogs sit where they do, and it is the first thing that will move when the demand pull comes back.

**First, we find out what people actually search for.** Everything above is a coverage comparison: it tells you where your competitors have built and you have not, and how valuable each of those places looks against your business, your costs and your existing traffic. What it cannot tell you is how many people are looking. That is a separate piece of work — a demand pull on the twelve gap topics — and until it comes back, no brief gets written and no writer starts. It also unlocks the two things this report had to leave blank: the fifth scoring factor, and the shortlist of cheap-and-wanted topics that the scoring model could not produce without it. Expect the order above to change when it lands, most likely in favour of winter, which is currently penalised for being crowded rather than judged on whether the crowd is there for a reason.

**Second, the pages you already have get sharpened before new ones go up.** Five of the twelve gaps are places where you have one page and a competitor has five or six. In three of those — boot care, map and compass, blister prevention — the right first move is not a new article but a narrowing of the page you have, so it stops half-covering four subjects and becomes the hub the new pages point at. That work is cheap, it protects traffic you already earn, and it makes each new page land better.

**Third, the new pages get written, in the order the demand pull leaves them in.** Two of them — the wild-camping law piece and any GPS-watch roundup — carry dependencies that can slip (a legal check, product access), so they are scheduled where a slip does not stall the month.

Winter and hiking-with-dogs look like the obvious answer and score near the bottom, because both competitors already hold that ground well and neither sits on gear you have shown us you sell. Tell us you stock winter kit and winter moves up a tier immediately.
