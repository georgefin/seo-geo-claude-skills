## Prioritized Gap List

### How these were scored

All five factors were scored — the export supplies volume, so **Search Demand has a real input this run and nothing was dropped or proxied.** Published weights apply throughout: Search Demand 0.25 · Competitive Density 0.20 · Business Relevance 0.25 · Creation Effort 0.15 · Conversion Potential 0.15, summing to 1.00.

**All five point the same way — 5 is always the value that favours you** — and all five are **added**, none subtracted. That is worth saying for the two that read as costs: **Creation Effort 5 = quick to create**, **Competitive Density 5 = no competitor covers it**.

**Search Demand bands.** The published bands give 1 = <100/month, 3 = 500–2,000, 5 = >5,000. The two intermediate scores are interpolated on a stated scale: **1 = <100 · 2 = 100–499 · 3 = 500–2,000 · 4 = 2,001–5,000 · 5 = >5,000.**

**Competitive Density bands.** Since no row here can score 5 (see the overlap note above), the scale used is stated: **4 = one competitor holds it, weakly (position 6+) · 3 = one competitor holds it at position 1–5, the other has no coverage of the topic · 2 = both competitors have topical coverage, ranker at position 4+ · 1 = both cover it and the ranker is top-3.**

**The demand gate ran, and everything passed it.** The quick-win screen is a demand test before it is a cost test: a keyword below the volume floor with no named proxy is not screened at all. The lowest volume in this export is 480/month, comfortably above the >100/month floor, so all eleven rows entered the screen and all eleven carry a Quick Win Score. No row scores Search Demand 1, so no row has a Quick Win Score it was not entitled to.

**Tier rule**: Tier 1 = P0 or P1 with a Quick Win Score of 2 or better · Tier 2 = P0 or P1 that missed that bar · Tier 3 = P2 and P3.
