## Prioritized Gap List

### How these gaps were scored, and what was left out

**Search Demand was dropped, not guessed.** The factor is banded in monthly search volume. No SEO tool is connected and no volume figures were supplied, so the factor has no input for this run.

The obvious substitute was **competitor cluster depth** — score demand from how many articles they have built on a topic. **That proxy is not admissible here**, and the reason is worth stating because it is not obvious: Competitive Density on every row below is scored from exactly that same competitor coverage, and Density is inverted (5 = nobody covers it). Feeding one body of evidence into both factors pushes them in opposite directions at comparable weights, so they largely cancel and five factors quietly behave as three. The ranking would look precise and would be carrying less information than it appears to.

Your own GA4 sessions on adjacent pages *would* have been an admissible proxy — they are independent of the competitor crawl. They were not used either, for a different reason: adjacent-page sessions exist for most of the depth gaps and for none of the two largest missing topics (you have no page adjacent to hiking-with-dogs or to winter), so proxying some rows and dropping the factor on others would put two differently-constructed numbers in one ranked list. Those session figures appear in the findings above as evidence you should read; they are deliberately not scored.

**So the factor was dropped and the remaining four weights renormalised over their own sum (0.75):**

| Factor | Published weight | Renormalised weight | Direction |
|---|---|---|---|
| ~~Search Demand~~ | ~~0.25~~ | **dropped — no input** | — |
| Competitive Density | 0.20 | 0.20 ÷ 0.75 = **0.2667** | 5 = no competitor covers it |
| Business Relevance | 0.25 | 0.25 ÷ 0.75 = **0.3333** | 5 = core to your offering |
| Creation Effort | 0.15 | 0.15 ÷ 0.75 = **0.2000** | 5 = quick to create |
| Conversion Potential | 0.15 | 0.15 ÷ 0.75 = **0.2000** | 5 = decision/transactional |
| | | **Sum 1.0000** | All four added, none subtracted |

Every score below is therefore `(20·Density + 25·Relevance + 15·Effort + 15·Conversion) ÷ 75`, printed as an exact fraction in fifteenths beside the figure rounded to two decimal places (halves up), with the tier read from the rounded figure.

**The quick-win screen was not run — Search Demand unavailable.** The Quick Win Score needs all four of its inputs and Search Demand is one of them; the three survivors would crown a gap nobody searches for simply for being cheap and uncontested. No Quick Win Score appears anywhere in this report. The gaps are ordered on the renormalised Gap Priority Score alone, and **P0 does the "start here" job**. Tier 1 below is therefore the P0 set, Tier 2 the P1 set, and Tier 3 the P2 and P3 set — the priority score alone, with no quick-win condition applied to any of them.

**One consequence of dropping Search Demand you should know about.** Competitive Density's share rose from 20% to 26.7%. Topics both rivals cover well are penalised harder than they would be under the published weights, and topics nobody has covered are rewarded harder. That is the correct behaviour for a run with no demand data — with no way to tell whether a crowded topic is crowded *because* it is in demand, "they are already there" is the strongest signal left — but it is why Winter and Dogs sit where they do, and it is the first thing that would move if you connect a keyword tool.
