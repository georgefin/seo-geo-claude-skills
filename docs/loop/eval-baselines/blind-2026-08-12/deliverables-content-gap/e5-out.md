## The short answer

**I cannot tell you what they cover that you don't, because you have given me the "they cover" half and not the "you don't" half.**

A content gap is a subtraction. It is *their topics minus your topics*, and both terms have to be real for the answer to be real. What I have is three competitor domain names. Even if I had a complete, accurate list of every page on all three — which I do not — the output would be **a list of things three companies write about**. That is not a gap analysis; it is a reading list. It becomes a gap the moment it is subtracted from your own content inventory, and your own content inventory is the one thing not in your message.

So the honest position is this: **there is one missing input, it is the load-bearing one, and it is the cheapest of everything on this list to produce** — you can compile it from your own CMS in an afternoon without buying a single tool. Send it and the full analysis runs.

**What I have not done, so you know what you are not getting:**

- **I have not visited the four sites**, so I cannot tell you their page counts, their topics or their formats. No crawler is connected and nothing was fetched.
- **I have not verified that sedumline.co.uk, greenlid-roofs.co.uk and urbancanopy-living.co.uk are your right competitors.** They are taken as given from your message. That assumption is worth checking before you spend effort on them — see the scope questions below.
- **I have not written you a plausible-looking gap list from general knowledge of the green-roof sector.** I could produce one that reads convincingly and every line of it would be invented. A list of topics a green-roof company "typically" covers, attached to three named businesses whose sites I have not seen, is a fabrication with your competitors' names on it. It would also be worse than useless in the specific way that matters: you would spend real writing budget against it.
- **I have not put a single number anywhere in this document.** No page counts, no traffic estimates, no search volumes, no priority scores. There is no data to compute any of them from, and a number with nothing behind it is the one output that cannot be corrected later, because it does not announce itself as wrong.

This is the input the analysis is built on. Every gap, every count, every score is defined against it. Nothing runs without it and nothing else on this list substitutes for it.

**Two notes on filling it in.** Leave the traffic column **empty** rather than estimating it — an empty column costs the analysis one section, a guessed column corrupts every priority score built on it. And use one topic label per page, consistently: "green roof maintenance" on four pages is worth more to this analysis than four differently-worded labels describing the same thing.

### 2. Competitor inventories — one per competitor

Same shape, less detail. You do not need traffic figures and you do not need every page; you need each competitor's **topic clusters and how many pages sit in each**, plus anything they publish that is not an article.

````markdown
<!-- SKELETON — a blank template for you to fill in, NOT a finished document.
     Every angle-bracket field is a placeholder. One copy per competitor. -->
# <competitor domain> — Content Inventory

**Compiled by**: <who> · **Date**: <YYYY-MM-DD> · **Method**: <manual crawl / sitemap read>
**Total content pages found**: <count, or "approximate — not exhaustive">

| Topic cluster | Number of articles | Titles you found |
|---------------|--------------------|------------------|
| <cluster name> | <count> | <title · title · title> |

**Standalone formats**: <videos / calculators / glossary / downloadable guides / case-study library / none found>
**Anything they have that surprised you**: <free text>
````

**Where to look**, in the order that is quickest: their blog or resources index (page through it to the end — the last page tells you the real total), their sitemap.xml if they have one, and their main navigation, which usually names their clusters for you. Record what you actually see. If the crawl is partial, **say what fraction you covered** — a count labelled "the first 40 of maybe 90" is usable; the same count presented as a total quietly becomes a false comparison, and every gap computed from it inherits the error.

### 3. Scope and goals — five short answers

Two of the four input checks for this analysis currently fail: no content inventory, and no stated business goals or priorities. The inventory is item 1 above; these are the rest.

````markdown
<!-- SKELETON — questions for you to answer in place, NOT a finished document. -->
**Your site**: verdantia-roofs.co.uk
**Competitors to analyse**: sedumline.co.uk · greenlid-roofs.co.uk · urbancanopy-living.co.uk
  → <confirm these three, or replace any that are not who you actually lose work to>
**Topic focus**: <everything, or a named area>
**Content types in scope**: <all, or e.g. "articles only — no video, we won't film">
**Audience**: <specifiers / architects / self-builders / commercial developers / homeowners — and
  which of them actually signs the contract>
**Business goal**: <what a successful page does — enquiry form, phone call, spec download, other>
**Geography**: <"across the UK" as stated — or is there a region you actually serve and quote in?>
````

Three of these change the output materially rather than decorating it. **Audience** decides Business Relevance, which is a quarter of every priority score. **Content types in scope** decides whether format gaps get reported as opportunities or as noise — there is no point ranking a video gap for a team that will not film. **Geography** decides whether a topic that looks national is really a set of regional pages.

### 4. Optional, and worth having if it is cheap for you

- **Analytics** — sessions per page for the last 90 days, from whatever you already have. Turns a flat page list into a weighted one, and lets the report show which of your existing pages a new cluster should hang off.
- **Search Console** — an impressions and positions export. This is the single upgrade that changes the *method* rather than the detail: it supplies the demand side, which is the factor the analysis otherwise has to drop.
- **Anything you already know** — where you win work, which enquiries keep arriving with the same question attached, what your competitors do that irritates you. This is real evidence about business relevance and it is free.

## What comes back, once the inputs arrive

With items 1–3 and no tools connected, the finished report contains:

- **Coverage comparison** — a topic matrix, your page counts against each of the three competitors', with wholly missing topics separated from topics you cover thinly and topics you cover from a different angle.
- **Gap findings** — grouped as missing topics, depth gaps, format gaps, buyer-journey gaps across four stages, and answer-engine opportunities. Every finding cites both sides: their coverage as observed, and its absence from your inventory, with the place each was counted.
- **A prioritised gap list** — each gap scored on business relevance, competitive density, creation effort and conversion potential, with the arithmetic printed beside every score so you can re-run it or disagree with a specific factor rather than the whole ranking.
- **A content calendar** — sequenced by priority and dependency, capped at the publishing capacity you state in item 1. If you tell me two articles a month, you get a plan for two articles a month.
- **Success metrics** — baselines that exist on the day, and the date each target gets set from real data.
- **A sources-and-limitations section** — every figure's origin, and every figure that is missing, named alongside what would supply it.

**Two things that report will not contain, whatever you send.** There will be **no traffic forecast** for pages that do not exist yet — searches are not visits, and the conversion between them depends on a ranking position and a click-through rate that nobody knows in advance. And there will be **no search-demand score** unless a keyword tool or Search Console is connected: that factor is measured in monthly searches, and with no source for them it gets dropped from the model and the remaining weights rescaled, with the rescaling shown. The gaps still rank; they rank on four factors instead of five, and the report says so on the page rather than in a footnote.

There is one shortcut, and it is honest about what it costs you. Send **only your own inventory** — item 1, which comes out of your own CMS — and name the three competitors. I can then produce a genuine analysis of *your* coverage: where your content is thin, where your topics are one page deep, which buyer-journey stages you have nothing at, and which answer-engine shapes you are missing. That is roughly half the value of the full report and it needs nothing from anybody else's website.

What it cannot give you is the thing you actually asked for. **"What they cover that we don't" needs both halves, and the competitor half has to be observed rather than assumed.** Nobody can shortcut that part, including me.
