`HTTP/1.1 200 Connection Established` is the proxy granting the tunnel. The `403` after it
is the origin's answer. `server: cloudflare`, `cf-mitigated: challenge`, a `chlray` timing
token and a CSP scoped to `challenges.cloudflare.com` identify it as a Cloudflare bot
challenge page (5,365 bytes of challenge HTML, not site content).

**Conclusion, stated plainly: the environment is correct this time.** `www.sanihellas.gr`
passes the egress allowlist. The blocker moved from the proxy to the site's own WAF.

### Why I did not get past it

Clearing a `cf-mitigated: challenge` requires either presenting a browser user-agent string
this client does not have, or executing the Cloudflare JS challenge. Both are detection
evasion. The task pins the default user agent and forbids retrying a 403, improvising, or
routing around a refusal — so the crawl stops here rather than being forced through. The
site is the owner's own property, but a crawler that lies about what it is would be the
wrong instrument regardless of who owns the target.

### What would unblock it (owner's decision, not taken here)

Three options, in descending order of how much they preserve the site's protection. None of
these were attempted:

1. **Allowlist the crawler at Cloudflare** — a WAF skip rule on this session's egress IP, or
   a shared secret header. Cleanest: the site keeps full bot protection for everyone else.
2. **Authorise a headless-browser crawl.** Chromium and Playwright are pre-installed here.
   A real browser is not a spoofed one — it would pass the challenge by being what the
   challenge tests for. This needs the owner's explicit say-so, since it changes the agreed
   crawl method, and it is slower and heavier on the live site.
3. **Owner-supplied export** — a `sitemap.xml` dump, a product-feed CSV, or a Screaming Frog
   export. No live crawling at all, and the fastest route to a candidate inventory.
