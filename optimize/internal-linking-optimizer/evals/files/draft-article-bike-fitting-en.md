## Draft body (verbatim)

Getting your riding position right is the cheapest upgrade in cycling. Before you spend on wheels, spend an evening on fit.

### Saddle height

Set the saddle so your knee keeps a slight bend at the bottom of the pedal stroke. A saddle set too low wastes power on every revolution; one set too high rocks your hips and chafes. Measure from the bottom bracket, change one thing at a time, and ride a week before judging.

### Reach and handlebar drop

If your shoulders ache after an hour, the bars are usually too far away or too low. Small stem changes beat big saddle changes — move in 10 mm steps.

### Cleat position

Start with the cleat under the ball of your foot and adjust in millimetres, not centimetres. Knee pain that appears only on the bike is very often a cleat problem, not a fitness problem.

### Fit only works on a maintained bike

None of this matters if the bike itself is neglected — a worn chain skips under load, and a dragging brake steals the watts a good position just saved you. If you are not sure where to start, book a professional service and ask for a fit check while the bike is on the stand. The basics you can do at home are all covered in our full bike maintenance guide.
